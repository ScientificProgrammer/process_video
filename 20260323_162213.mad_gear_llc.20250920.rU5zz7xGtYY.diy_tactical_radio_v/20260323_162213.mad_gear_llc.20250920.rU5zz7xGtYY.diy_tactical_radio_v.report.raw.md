## Executive Summary

# Open MANET vs Military-Grade MANET Radios: A Comprehensive Comparison

## Overview

This video provides an in-depth comparison between expensive military-grade MANET (Mobile Ad Hoc Networking) radios and the affordable, open-source Open MANET project. The creator clarifies misconceptions from a viral video about reverse-engineering military routers and explains how the DIY community is developing accessible alternatives for civilian community resilience networks.

## Introduction to MANET Technology

The video addresses a viral video about reverse-engineering a $20,000 military MANET radio and introduces the Open MANET project as a community-driven alternative. The creator emphasizes this is purely educational content comparing military-grade equipment with DIY solutions.

## Military-Grade MANET Radios

### Top-Tier Systems

Two primary manufacturers dominate the military MANET radio market:

- **Silvus Technologies**: Streamcaster radio system
- **Persistent Systems**: MPU5 radio system
- Both systems cost five figures (minimum $10,000+) on the secondary market

### Key Features of Military Systems

**Proprietary Waveforms:**
- Custom-built radio waves with specific capabilities
- Low Probability of Intercept (LPI)
- Low Probability of Detection (LPD)
- Anti-jam properties
- Persistent Systems uses Wave Relay technology
- Silvus uses MN-MIMO (Mobile Networked Multiple Input Multiple Output)

**Integrated Ecosystem:**
- Complete "it just works" solution
- Integrated software and user interface
- Interconnected radio systems
- Mission-critical reliability

**Premium Hardware:**
- IP68 rated enclosures (water and dust resistant)
- Swappable frequency packs
- Ruggedized construction
- Designed to withstand extreme conditions

### Do You Actually Need Military-Grade Equipment?

The creator argues that unless issued for professional military use, most people don't need these expensive systems. The comparison is made to buying a Ferrari as a grocery vehicle - impressive but impractical. These features are designed for contested military environments, not civilian community resilience applications.

## Open MANET Project

### Project Overview

Open MANET is a community-driven, open-source project developing affordable MANET equipment:

- Currently in early development stages
- Primarily DIY-focused
- Built by volunteers in their spare time
- Aims to fill the gap between no solution and $20,000 military systems

### The "Recipe" Approach

Open MANET is described as a recipe rather than a finished product, using off-the-shelf components and open-source software.

### Software Components

**OpenWRT:**
- Linux-based, lightweight operating system
- Highly customizable for networking devices
- Transforms devices into routers with advanced configuration

**Batman Advanced:**
- Stands for Better Approach To Mobile Ad hoc Networking
- Enables intelligent routing
- Nodes continuously evaluate connection quality to neighbors
- Self-healing mesh network capability
- Automatically reroutes traffic when nodes go down

### Hardware Components

**Raspberry Pi:**
- Single-board computer used as the testbed platform
- Allows for specific modifications and additions

**Wi-Fi HaLow (802.11ah):**
- Operates in the 900 MHz frequency band
- Significantly extended range (miles vs. hundreds of feet)
- Superior penetration through walls and foliage
- Better than traditional 2.4 GHz or 5 GHz Wi-Fi for distance

### IEEE Standards vs. Proprietary Waveforms

Open MANET uses open IEEE standards (802.11ah, 802.11s) that anyone can implement, contrasting with proprietary military waveforms. This open approach aligns with the project's accessibility goals.

## Head-to-Head Comparison

### Data Throughput

**Military Systems:**
- Hundreds of megabytes per second

**Open MANET:**
- 1-15 megabytes per second (optimal conditions)
- Sufficient for most civilian applications:
  - Sending pictures
  - Data packages
  - Map updates (ATAK)
  - Single video streaming
  - Adequate for 99% of community resilience needs

### Security Features

**Military Systems:**
- Military-grade encryption
- LPI/LPD capabilities
- Anti-jam technology

**Open MANET:**
- Standard Wi-Fi security
- Sufficient for most civilian use cases
- Adequate unless facing nation-state level threats

### Voice and Video Integration

**Military Systems:**
- Native hardware video encoders
- Built-in push-to-talk functionality

**Open MANET:**
- Software-based transmission
- Less efficient than dedicated hardware encoders

### Network Scalability

**Military Systems:**
- Support hundreds of nodes
- Extensively tested for reliability
- Proven in dynamic environments

**Open MANET:**
- Supports multiple nodes for community use
- Less extensive testing
- Limited by volunteer development resources
- Suitable for 99% of civilian applications

### Hardware Durability

**Military Systems:**
- IP68 rated
- Extremely ruggedized
- Can withstand being dropped, run over
- Designed for harsh environments

**Open MANET:**
- 3D-printed enclosures designed by community
- Not water or dust resistant
- Not metal construction
- Adequate for most civilian use cases
- Improving as community develops solutions

## Cost Comparison

The most significant advantage of Open MANET is accessibility:

- **Military Systems**: $10,000-$20,000+ per unit
- **Open MANET**: Approximately $100 to get started
- All information freely available on GitHub

## Conclusion and Call to Action

Open MANET devices are not reverse-engineered military routers but rather a different approach to similar networking challenges. The project represents an incredibly valuable middle-ground solution that doesn't currently exist in the commercial market.

**Key Takeaways:**
- Open MANET drives innovation in affordable mesh networking
- Creates accessible DIY knowledge for communities
- Encourages tinkering and community involvement
- Makes resilient communication technology available to everyone
- Will inspire commercial solutions in the mid-range market

The creator encourages viewers to support the Open MANET project and get involved in the open-source community to help advance accessible networking technology for civilian community resilience applications.

## Reference

### Key Terms Glossary

# GLOSSARY OF TERMS

- MANET (Mobile Ad Hoc Networking): A self-configuring network of mobile devices connected wirelessly where each device can move independently and communicate directly with other devices without requiring fixed infrastructure like cell towers or routers. The network automatically reorganizes itself as devices move, join, or leave, creating a dynamic mesh of connections.
-- Analogy: A group of walkie-talkie users who can relay messages through each other to reach someone out of direct range, with the relay paths automatically adjusting as people move around.
-- Why It Matters: MANET enables resilient communications in areas without traditional infrastructure, crucial for military operations, disaster response, and community resilience.

- PROPRIETARY WAVEFORM: A custom-designed radio signal pattern created by a specific company that uses unique modulation, encoding, and transmission techniques not based on public standards. These waveforms are engineered to achieve specific performance goals like security, range, or resistance to interference that standard protocols cannot provide.
-- Analogy: A secret handshake that only members of an exclusive club know, versus the standard wave everyone uses to say hello.
-- Why It Matters: Proprietary waveforms enable advanced military features like anti-jamming and stealth but lock users into expensive, vendor-specific ecosystems.

- LPI/LPD (Low Probability of Intercept/Low Probability of Detection): Radio transmission techniques that make signals extremely difficult for adversaries to detect or intercept by spreading the signal across frequencies, using low power, or employing other methods to hide the transmission in background noise. These methods prevent enemies from knowing that communication is occurring or where it is coming from.
-- Analogy: Whispering in a crowded, noisy room so quietly that eavesdroppers cannot tell you are talking or locate where the whisper is coming from.
-- Why It Matters: Critical for military operations where revealing your position or the fact that you are communicating could be fatal.

- MN-MIMO (Mobile Networked Multiple Input Multiple Output): A radio technology that uses multiple antennas on both transmitting and receiving devices while nodes are moving within a network, allowing simultaneous data streams that increase throughput and reliability. The "mobile networked" aspect means these benefits apply across a mesh of moving devices, not just point-to-point links.
-- Analogy: Multiple people shouting different parts of a message simultaneously through different megaphones to multiple listeners with multiple ears, all while everyone is walking around.
-- Why It Matters: Dramatically increases data capacity and connection reliability in mobile military or tactical scenarios where devices are constantly moving.

- IP68 RATING: An international standard rating indicating a device is completely dust-tight (the 6) and can survive continuous immersion in water beyond 1 meter depth (the 8), typically meaning the device can withstand harsh environmental conditions. This rating ensures equipment will function reliably in extreme weather, mud, dust storms, or if dropped in water.
-- Analogy: A waterproof watch that you can wear while swimming in the ocean versus a regular watch that breaks if it gets wet.
-- Why It Matters: Essential for military and emergency equipment that must function reliably regardless of environmental conditions.

- OpenWRT: A Linux-based open-source operating system designed specifically for network routers and embedded devices that replaces manufacturer firmware with highly customizable software. It provides advanced networking features, extensive configuration options, and the ability to install additional software packages that consumer router firmware does not support.
-- Analogy: Replacing the basic dashboard in your car with a fully programmable computer system that lets you customize and control every aspect of the vehicle.
-- Why It Matters: Enables hobbyists and developers to turn inexpensive consumer hardware into powerful, feature-rich networking devices with capabilities rivaling professional equipment.

- BATMAN ADVANCED (Better Approach To Mobile Ad hoc Networking): A routing protocol that operates at the network layer to intelligently direct data packets through a mesh network by having each node continuously measure the quality of connections to neighboring nodes. The protocol automatically finds the best path for data and reroutes around failed or degraded connections without human intervention.
-- Analogy: A GPS navigation system that constantly monitors traffic conditions on all roads and automatically reroutes you around accidents or congestion in real-time.
-- Why It Matters: Enables self-healing mesh networks that maintain connectivity even as nodes move, fail, or new nodes join, critical for resilient communications.

- WI-FI HALOW (802.11ah): A Wi-Fi standard operating in the sub-1 GHz frequency band (specifically 900 MHz) designed for long-range, low-power applications with better obstacle penetration than traditional Wi-Fi. It trades higher data speeds for significantly extended range, measured in miles rather than feet, and improved ability to pass through walls and foliage.
-- Analogy: A fog horn that can be heard for miles versus a regular voice that only carries a few hundred feet, though the fog horn cannot convey complex speech as quickly.
-- Why It Matters: Enables Wi-Fi-based mesh networks to cover much larger areas with fewer nodes, making community-scale networks practical and affordable.

- MESH NETWORK: A network topology where each node connects to multiple other nodes, creating redundant pathways for data so that if one node fails, data automatically reroutes through alternative paths. Unlike traditional networks with central hubs, mesh networks distribute routing intelligence across all nodes, making them highly resilient.
-- Analogy: A spider web where removing one strand does not break the web because the silk connects in multiple directions, versus a bicycle wheel where cutting one spoke weakens the entire structure.
-- Why It Matters: Provides network resilience without single points of failure, essential for emergency communications and areas without reliable infrastructure.

- ATAK (Android Team Awareness Kit): A situational awareness application developed by the U.S. military that displays real-time mapping, friendly force locations, imagery, and tactical data on Android devices. It enables teams to share location data, mark points of interest, coordinate movements, and maintain common operational pictures across distributed units.
-- Analogy: A multiplayer video game map that shows where all your teammates are, what they are looking at, and lets you mark objectives, but for real-world tactical operations.
-- Why It Matters: Demonstrates practical applications for mesh networks in coordinating distributed teams for military, emergency response, or community resilience scenarios.

- IEEE 802.11 STANDARDS: A family of international specifications for wireless local area network (WLAN) technologies developed by the Institute of Electrical and Electronics Engineers that define how Wi-Fi devices communicate. Different letter suffixes (like 802.11ah, ax, be) indicate different generations with varying frequencies, speeds, and capabilities that any manufacturer can implement.
-- Analogy: Standardized railroad track gauges that allow any train from any manufacturer to run on the same rails, versus proprietary tracks that only work with one company's trains.
-- Why It Matters: Open standards enable interoperability between devices from different manufacturers and allow open-source projects to build compatible equipment without licensing fees.

- ANTI-JAM: Radio technologies and techniques designed to maintain communications even when adversaries deliberately transmit interference signals attempting to block or disrupt the radio frequencies being used. Methods include frequency hopping, spread spectrum, adaptive filtering, and high-powered transmission to overcome jamming signals.
-- Analogy: A conversation that continues by rapidly switching between different languages faster than a disruptor can learn them, or shouting loud enough to be heard over someone trying to drown you out.
-- Why It Matters: Essential for military communications in contested environments where enemies actively attempt to deny radio communications.

- NODES: Individual devices or connection points in a network that can receive, create, store, or forward data to other nodes. In mesh networks, each node acts as both a client using the network and a router helping other nodes communicate, with the network's coverage and capacity growing as more nodes are added.
-- Analogy: People in a bucket brigade passing water to fight a fire, where each person is both receiving buckets and passing them along to others.
-- Why It Matters: Understanding nodes is fundamental to grasping how mesh networks scale, self-heal, and provide coverage through distributed rather than centralized architecture.

- THROUGHPUT: The actual amount of data successfully transferred through a network connection over a specific time period, typically measured in megabytes per second (MB/s) or megabits per second (Mbps). Throughput differs from theoretical maximum speed because it accounts for real-world factors like interference, protocol overhead, and network congestion.
-- Analogy: The actual number of cars that successfully drive through a tunnel per hour, which is less than the theoretical maximum because of traffic, accidents, and toll booth delays.
-- Why It Matters: Determines what applications are practical on a network, from simple text messaging requiring minimal throughput to video streaming requiring much higher sustained data rates.
