# Open MANET vs Military-Grade MANET Radios: A Comprehensive Comparison

## Overview

This video provides an in-depth comparison between expensive military-grade MANET (Mobile Ad Hoc Networking) radios and the affordable, open-source Open MANET project. The creator clarifies misconceptions from a viral video about reverse-engineering military routers and explains how the DIY community is developing accessible alternatives for civilian community resilience networks.

## Introduction to MANET Technology

The video addresses a viral video about reverse-engineering a $20,000 military MANET radio and introduces the Open MANET project as a community-driven alternative. The creator emphasizes this is purely educational content comparing military-grade equipment with DIY solutions.

## Military-Grade MANET Radios

### Top-Tier Systems

Two primary manufacturers dominate the military MANET radio market:

- **Silvus Technologies**: Streamcaster radio system
- **Persistent Systems**: MPU5 radio system
- Both systems cost five figures (minimum $10,000+) on the secondary market

### Key Features of Military Systems

**Proprietary Waveforms:**
- Custom-built radio waves with specific capabilities
- Low Probability of Intercept (LPI)
- Low Probability of Detection (LPD)
- Anti-jam properties
- Persistent Systems uses Wave Relay technology
- Silvus uses MN-MIMO (Mobile Networked Multiple Input Multiple Output)

**Integrated Ecosystem:**
- Complete "it just works" solution
- Integrated software and user interface
- Interconnected radio systems
- Mission-critical reliability

**Premium Hardware:**
- IP68 rated enclosures (water and dust resistant)
- Swappable frequency packs
- Ruggedized construction
- Designed to withstand extreme conditions

### Do You Actually Need Military-Grade Equipment?

The creator argues that unless issued for professional military use, most people don't need these expensive systems. The comparison is made to buying a Ferrari as a grocery vehicle - impressive but impractical. These features are designed for contested military environments, not civilian community resilience applications.

## Open MANET Project

### Project Overview

Open MANET is a community-driven, open-source project developing affordable MANET equipment:

- Currently in early development stages
- Primarily DIY-focused
- Built by volunteers in their spare time
- Aims to fill the gap between no solution and $20,000 military systems

### The "Recipe" Approach

Open MANET is described as a recipe rather than a finished product, using off-the-shelf components and open-source software.

### Software Components

**OpenWRT:**
- Linux-based, lightweight operating system
- Highly customizable for networking devices
- Transforms devices into routers with advanced configuration

**Batman Advanced:**
- Stands for Better Approach To Mobile Ad hoc Networking
- Enables intelligent routing
- Nodes continuously evaluate connection quality to neighbors
- Self-healing mesh network capability
- Automatically reroutes traffic when nodes go down

### Hardware Components

**Raspberry Pi:**
- Single-board computer used as the testbed platform
- Allows for specific modifications and additions

**Wi-Fi HaLow (802.11ah):**
- Operates in the 900 MHz frequency band
- Significantly extended range (miles vs. hundreds of feet)
- Superior penetration through walls and foliage
- Better than traditional 2.4 GHz or 5 GHz Wi-Fi for distance

### IEEE Standards vs. Proprietary Waveforms

Open MANET uses open IEEE standards (802.11ah, 802.11s) that anyone can implement, contrasting with proprietary military waveforms. This open approach aligns with the project's accessibility goals.

## Head-to-Head Comparison

### Data Throughput

**Military Systems:**
- Hundreds of megabytes per second

**Open MANET:**
- 1-15 megabytes per second (optimal conditions)
- Sufficient for most civilian applications:
  - Sending pictures
  - Data packages
  - Map updates (ATAK)
  - Single video streaming
  - Adequate for 99% of community resilience needs

### Security Features

**Military Systems:**
- Military-grade encryption
- LPI/LPD capabilities
- Anti-jam technology

**Open MANET:**
- Standard Wi-Fi security
- Sufficient for most civilian use cases
- Adequate unless facing nation-state level threats

### Voice and Video Integration

**Military Systems:**
- Native hardware video encoders
- Built-in push-to-talk functionality

**Open MANET:**
- Software-based transmission
- Less efficient than dedicated hardware encoders

### Network Scalability

**Military Systems:**
- Support hundreds of nodes
- Extensively tested for reliability
- Proven in dynamic environments

**Open MANET:**
- Supports multiple nodes for community use
- Less extensive testing
- Limited by volunteer development resources
- Suitable for 99% of civilian applications

### Hardware Durability

**Military Systems:**
- IP68 rated
- Extremely ruggedized
- Can withstand being dropped, run over
- Designed for harsh environments

**Open MANET:**
- 3D-printed enclosures designed by community
- Not water or dust resistant
- Not metal construction
- Adequate for most civilian use cases
- Improving as community develops solutions

## Cost Comparison

The most significant advantage of Open MANET is accessibility:

- **Military Systems**: $10,000-$20,000+ per unit
- **Open MANET**: Approximately $100 to get started
- All information freely available on GitHub

## Conclusion and Call to Action

Open MANET devices are not reverse-engineered military routers but rather a different approach to similar networking challenges. The project represents an incredibly valuable middle-ground solution that doesn't currently exist in the commercial market.

**Key Takeaways:**
- Open MANET drives innovation in affordable mesh networking
- Creates accessible DIY knowledge for communities
- Encourages tinkering and community involvement
- Makes resilient communication technology available to everyone
- Will inspire commercial solutions in the mid-range market
