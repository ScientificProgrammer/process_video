# ONE SENTENCE SUMMARY:

Open MANET provides affordable DIY mesh networking alternatives to expensive military-grade MANET radios for community resilience applications.

# MAIN POINTS:

1. Military MANET radios from Silvus and Persistent Systems cost five figures with proprietary waveforms.
2. High-end systems offer low probability intercept, detection, anti-jam capabilities, and integrated ecosystems.
3. Most civilians don't need military-grade features designed for contested battlefield environments with adversaries.
4. Open MANET is a community-driven project developing affordable MANET gear using open-source components.
5. The project uses OpenWRT software and Batman advanced protocol for self-healing mesh networks.
6. Wi-Fi HaLow operates at 900 MHz, providing miles of range with better penetration.
7. Military radios achieve hundreds of megabytes per second versus Open MANET's 1-15 megabytes.
8. Open MANET uses Raspberry Pi hardware with 3D-printed enclosures instead of ruggedized IP68 cases.
9. Standard Wi-Fi security on Open MANET suffices for most users not facing nation-state threats.
10. Open MANET costs around one hundred dollars to start versus thousands for military equipment.

# TAKEAWAYS:

1. Open MANET fills the market gap between expensive military radios and non-existent affordable options.
2. Community mesh networks for resilience don't require military-grade encryption or anti-jam capabilities typically.
3. 1-15 megabytes per second bandwidth handles pictures, data, map updates, and video streaming adequately.
4. Open-source development relies on volunteer time and creates accessible knowledge for everyone interested.
5. Supporting Open MANET advances affordable networking technology and drives innovation in underserved markets.
