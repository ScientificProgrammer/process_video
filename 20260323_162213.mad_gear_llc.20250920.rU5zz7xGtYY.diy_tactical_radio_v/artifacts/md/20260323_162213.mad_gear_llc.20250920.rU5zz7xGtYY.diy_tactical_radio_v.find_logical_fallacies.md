# FALLACIES

- Appeal to Probability: Informal Fallacy — Assumes Open MANET will inevitably improve with community involvement without concrete evidence of timeline.

- Hasty Generalization: Faulty Generalization — Claims 99% of people will find 1-15 Mbps sufficient without supporting statistical evidence.

- Appeal to Accomplishment: Red Herring — Suggests military systems developed by RF engineers with years of experience are superior based on credentials.

- False Analogy: Faulty Generalization — Compares buying military MANET radio to buying Ferrari as grocery getter without addressing relevant differences.

- Bandwagon Appeal: Red Herring — Encourages viewers to support Open MANET and join community based on collective participation rather than merit.

- Appeal to Poverty: Red Herring — Implies Open MANET is better partly because it costs $100 versus expensive military alternatives.

- Anecdotal Evidence: Faulty Generalization — Uses personal experience in community as evidence that people are working hard to improve project.

- Appeal to Novelty: Red Herring — Suggests upcoming technology will be really cool without specifying what makes it valuable or improved.

- Incomplete Comparison: Informal Fallacy — States 900 MHz comes at a cost but delays explaining what that cost is.

- Argument from Silence: Relevance Fallacy — Claims there aren't many middle-market solutions without providing evidence of market research or alternatives.

- Appeal to Emotion: Red Herring — Uses phrase this is probably going to piss some people off to emotionally prime audience.

- Proof by Assertion: Informal Fallacy — Repeatedly states Open MANET is good enough for most uses without comprehensive comparative testing data.
