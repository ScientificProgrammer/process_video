# Controversial Ideas

- Most civilians don't need expensive military-grade MANET radios despite what enthusiasts might believe or want.
- Buying $20,000 military communication equipment for civilian use is impractical like buying a Ferrari for groceries.
- Standard Wi-Fi security is sufficient for most people unless facing nation-state level adversaries directly.
- Open-source DIY communication networks can adequately replace military-grade systems for 99% of civilian users.
- Community resilience networks don't require military-grade encryption, low probability intercept, or anti-jam capabilities practically.
- Open MANET's 1-15 megabytes per second is adequate for 99% of people's communication needs realistically.
- Military-grade features are designed for contested environments that civilians will most likely never experience.
- Civilians are paying massive premiums for capabilities in military radios they will most likely never use.
- Open-source projects with 3D-printed enclosures can meet most users' needs despite lacking military ruggedization.
- A $100 open-source solution can provide similar functionality to $20,000 military communication systems for civilians.
- The viral video claiming reverse engineering of military routers is misleading about the actual technology involved.
- Open-source communities can democratize military-level communication technology without expensive proprietary systems or government involvement.
