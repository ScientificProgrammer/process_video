## Executive Summary

This video compares expensive military-grade MANET (Mobile Ad Hoc Networking) radios with the affordable, open-source Open MANET project. The creator addresses a viral video about reverse-engineering military routers and clarifies how the DIY community develops accessible alternatives for civilian community resilience networks.

**Military-Grade MANET Systems:**
- Silvus Technologies (Streamcaster) and Persistent Systems (MPU5) dominate the market
- Cost five figures minimum ($10,000+) on secondary markets
- Feature proprietary waveforms with Low Probability of Intercept/Detection (LPI/LPD) and anti-jam capabilities
- Provide integrated ecosystems with seamless software, interfaces, and hardware
- Offer IP68-rated ruggedized enclosures and swappable frequency packs
- Achieve hundreds of megabytes per second throughput
- Support hundreds of nodes with extensive reliability testing

**Open MANET Project:**
- Community-driven, open-source alternative in early development
- Built by volunteers using spare time without significant funding
- Uses OpenWRT operating system and Batman Advanced routing protocol for self-healing mesh networks
- Employs Raspberry Pi hardware with Wi-Fi HaLow (802.11ah) operating at 900 MHz
- Provides 1-15 megabytes per second throughput—sufficient for pictures, data packages, map updates (ATAK), and video streaming
- Costs approximately $100 to start versus $20,000+ for military systems
- Uses standard Wi-Fi security adequate for non-nation-state threats
- Features 3D-printed enclosures designed by community members

**Key Argument:**
Most civilians don't need military-grade features designed for contested environments. The comparison is like buying a Ferrari for grocery shopping—impressive but impractical. Open MANET fills the market gap between no solution and expensive military systems, providing adequate capability for 99% of community resilience applications while democratizing access to resilient communication technology.

### Technology Impact Analysis

# TECHNOLOGY IMPACT ANALYSIS

## SUMMARY

Open MANET is a community-driven open-source project developing affordable Mobile Ad Hoc Networking radio systems for civilian resilience using accessible hardware and software.

## TECHNOLOGIES USED

- OpenWRT (Linux-based networking operating system)
- Batman Advanced (mesh networking protocol)
- Wi-Fi HaLow (802.11ah standard at 900 MHz)
- Raspberry Pi single-board computers
- IEEE 802.11s mesh networking standard
- 3D-printed enclosures
- Standard Wi-Fi security protocols

## TARGET AUDIENCE

- Community resilience groups and emergency preparedness organizations
- DIY technology enthusiasts and makers
- Individuals seeking affordable mesh networking solutions
- Open-source development communities
- Civilians requiring reliable local communications without military-grade budgets

## OUTCOMES

- Provides MANET capabilities at ~$100 versus $20,000 military alternatives
- Enables 1-15 megabytes per second throughput sufficient for situational awareness
- Supports community-scale mesh networks with self-healing capabilities
- Creates accessible documentation through GitHub
- Demonstrates viable alternative to proprietary military systems
- Facilitates picture sharing, data packages, map updates, and video streaming
- Extends communication range to miles versus hundreds of feet

## SOCIETAL IMPACT

**Positive Impacts:**

Open MANET democratizes resilient communication technology previously available only to military and well-funded organizations. The 100x-200x cost reduction enables community-based emergency preparedness and disaster response. Open-source nature promotes knowledge sharing, skill development, and technological literacy. Communities can establish independent communication infrastructure that functions when traditional networks fail, enhancing local resilience and self-sufficiency.

The project fosters innovation by creating a middle-market solution space, potentially spurring commercial development. It builds technical communities where individuals collaborate, learn, and contribute. Transparency allows community oversight and continuous improvement.

**Negative Impacts:**

DIY nature means systems lack reliability, testing, and ruggedization of professional equipment, potentially creating false confidence in emergencies. Users may overestimate capabilities, leading to communication failures during critical moments. Technical knowledge requirements may exclude less technically-inclined members, creating digital divides.

Without professional support, troubleshooting and maintenance fall entirely on users. Volunteer development means inconsistent progress, potential security vulnerabilities, and lack of guaranteed updates. Standard Wi-Fi security, while adequate for most civilian uses, could be compromised by sophisticated actors.

## ETHICAL CONSIDERATIONS

The project presents minimal ethical concerns. Primary consideration involves ensuring users understand system limitations to prevent over-reliance during emergencies. Transparency about capabilities versus military-grade systems is essential.

Potential dual-use concern exists, as communication technologies can enable both beneficial community resilience and potentially harmful coordination. However, this risk is inherent to all communication technologies.

Open-source nature promotes ethical values of transparency, accessibility, and knowledge democratization. The project positions itself as community resilience infrastructure rather than tactical military equipment, maintaining appropriate civilian focus.

Privacy considerations exist with any mesh networking system, requiring clear communication about data transmission and potential interception.

**Ethical Concern Severity: LOW**

The project demonstrates responsible development with appropriate transparency, focusing on civilian community benefit rather than militarization.
