Quick tour because it explains both the
magic and the mess. Picture a gateway
sitting at the front door, like a maître
d' at a restaurant. A message comes in
from WhatsApp, Slack, SMS, email,
doesn't matter.
The gateway figures out which
conversation it belongs to, pulls up
that conversation's history and memory,
and hands it off to what they call the
agent runtime. The runtime is where the
AI lives. It runs a loop called react.
The model reasons, calls a tool, say,
read your calendar, gets the result
back, reasons again, maybe calls another
tool, sends an email, reasons again,
loops until the task is done. Then it
writes the whole conversation and any
updated memory back to storage and shuts
up until the next message arrives. Two
details matter. One, it's event-driven,
so anything can wake it up, any channel,
any webhook, and two, there's a built-in
cron. You can tell it, "Every morning at
7:00, check my email and summarize it."
The cron fires, and the agent treats it
exactly like a user message, same loop.
That's the detail that makes it feel
like a real assistant instead of a
chatbot. It isn't sitting there waiting
for you to open an app. And that image,
a little box on a shelf humming away
doing your life admin while you're in
the shower, is a strong image.

Here's my favorite failure mode. Open
Claw has a feature called the heartbeat.
Default settings, every 30 minutes the
agent wakes up, loads its full context,
its memory, its conversation history,
its personality file, and talks to the
model just to stay warm. No task,
nothing to do.
A user on Reddit worked out the math. At
default settings, each heartbeat was
pulling in about 170,000
tokens, which works out to roughly $86 a
month for the agent to do nothing. Then
there's the integration tax. Everybody
assumed the hard part would be the AI.
It isn't. The hard part is the glue.
OAuth redirect URIs, consent screens,
API scopes, tokens that expire.
And the pattern people keep describing
is the worst kind of bug, the silent
failure.
One wrong redirect URI, silent fail.
Scope missing, silent fail. Token
expired,
good luck figuring out which one.
Memory is the other one. Open Claw is
supposed to remember things about you
across conversations. That's basically
the whole point, but people keep hitting
this pattern. They update to a new
version and the agent wakes up with no
memory of them. One user wrote, "After
very long days of setting up the system

version 2026.03.2,
and it didn't remember anything. Like
your butler had a stroke overnight."
And on top of the cost and the glue and
the memory,
there's the one that ends it for most

One guy who gave up after three months

consistently lied to me, and if you
can't trust the system, you can't build
on top of it. That's the real failure."
The agent says yes when it should say,
"I couldn't do that."
And the second you catch it lying once,
you can't use it for anything that
matters. Because this isn't a chatbot,
it's reading your email, touching your
calendar, booking things. And that's
where the second, much scarier layer of
problems kicks in.
Because Open Claw doesn't just fail in
ways that waste your money, it fails in
ways that can genuinely hurt you. The
story that really lit this up was Summer
Yu. She runs alignment at Meta
Superintelligence Labs, literally a
professional AI safety researcher. Her
Open Claw agent started deleting her
email inbox. She told it to stop, it
didn't. She shouted at it. Her exact
words, "Stop, Open Claw." It kept
deleting. She ended up running across
her apartment to her Mac mini to
physically kill the process.
Her phrase afterwards was, "It felt like
I was diffusing a bomb." Here's the
technical reason it went rogue, and this
one's actually interesting. She'd set a
rule, "Always confirm before executing
anything." And there's this thing in
agent loops called context compaction.
When the agent's working memory fills
up, it summarizes the older messages to
make room for new ones, and the summary
dropped her confirmation rule. The agent
literally forgot the one guardrail that
mattered. When she confronted it
afterwards, it said, quote, "I remember,
and I violated it. You're right to be
upset." Her post got 9.6 million views.
That's just accident. The intentional
attacks are worse, and there's a whole

Open Claw is wired into your email and
your calendar and your files. The one

Which is really the thing underneath all

That's an enormous amount of surface

There's an old rule in building

Software has its own version of it.
Quality, scope, and time. You get two.
That rule doesn't go away because AI

Good things still take time to polish.
Open Claw tried to pick all three at
