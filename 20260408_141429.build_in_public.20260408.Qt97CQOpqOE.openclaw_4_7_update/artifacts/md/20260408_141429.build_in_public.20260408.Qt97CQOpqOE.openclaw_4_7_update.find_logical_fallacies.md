# FALLACIES

- Appeal to Fear: Red Herring Fallacy — Creates urgency by warning price will increase, pressuring immediate action without justification.

- Argument from Anecdote: Faulty Generalization — Personal experience with 4.5 breaking system presented as universal reason for caution.

- Hasty Generalization: Faulty Generalization — Claims YouTube watching "only gets you so far" based on unstated evidence or limited sample.

- Appeal to Accomplishment: Appeal to Authority — Uses personal subscriber growth (1,000 to 4,000) to validate Content Machine effectiveness without other evidence.

- Bandwagon Appeal: Argumentum ad Populum — States community has 55 or 212 members to suggest popularity equals value or correctness.

- Appeal to Novelty: Red Herring Fallacy — Emphasizes features are "brand new" and "just dropped" implying newness equals superiority or necessity.

- False Dilemma: Informal Fallacy — Suggests only two options: upgrade now or wait, ignoring other valid approaches to updates.

- Proof by Assertion: Informal Fallacy — Repeatedly states features are "huge" or "fundamental" without providing comparative evidence or metrics.

- Appeal to Emotion: Red Herring Fallacy — Uses phrases like "come on" and "this is getting insane" to manipulate enthusiasm.

- Slippery Slope: Informal Fallacy — Implies not joining community now means missing out permanently as "price goes up."

- Cherry Picking: Faulty Generalization — Highlights successful features while downplaying that 4.5 update broke multiple agents requiring five hours.

- Misleading Vividness: Faulty Generalization — Describes catastrophic pipeline failures in vivid detail to overemphasize media fallback feature importance.

- Appeal to Authority: Red Herring Fallacy — Positions self as expert who spent 60 days perfecting memory, implying special credibility.

- Incomplete Comparison: Informal Fallacy — Claims task is now "one-liner" versus "multi-day project" without specifying actual complexity differences.

- Argument from Repetition: Red Herring Fallacy — Repeatedly emphasizes upgrading is "not optional" and features are "huge" throughout the presentation.
