# FALLACIES

- Appeal to Fear: Red Herring — AI industry claims regulation will kill entire industry to generate distress and anxiety.

- Slippery Slope: Improper Premise — Claiming state regulation creates chaotic patchwork that will inevitably crush all innovation completely.

- False Dilemma: Faulty Generalization — Presenting choice as either no regulation or slowing down AI when other options exist.

- Ad Hominem: Red Herring — OpenAI accused critics of being Elon Musk's puppets rather than addressing their arguments.

- Appeal to Consequences: Red Herring — Arguing regulation is bad because it will slow AI development at inopportune time.

- Straw Man: Relevance Fallacy — Tech lobby claimed child safety bill would end all chatbot access for children entirely.

- Argumentum ad Baculum: Red Herring — Threatening to leave California and sue states to coerce withdrawal of AI legislation.

- Association Fallacy: Relevance Fallacy — Implying critics are fronts for competitors simply because competitors also oppose OpenAI's restructuring.

- Appeal to Wealth: Red Herring — Spending 100 million dollars as message to intimidate candidates from taking regulatory positions.

- Appeal to Authority: Red Herring — Hiring Chris Lehane as Master of Disaster implies his reputation validates OpenAI's aggressive tactics.

- Bandwagon: Red Herring — Tech lobby spokesman claims Chamber of Progress independence based on board structure alone.

- Red Herring: Relevance Fallacy — Chamber of Progress spokesman deflects funding question by discussing voting procedures instead of answering.

- False Equivalence: Faulty Generalization — AI companies want social media liability immunity despite different technological and harm profiles.

- Appeal to Novelty: Red Herring — Implicit argument that AI innovation is so important it shouldn't face traditional regulatory scrutiny.

- Moving the Goalposts: Improper Premise — Governor signed weakened bill requiring protocols not guarantees after strong bill passed chambers.

- Nirvana Fallacy: Improper Premise — Dismissing copyright as wrong lever implies perfect solution exists or none should apply.

- Proving Too Much: Improper Premise — If complying with 50 state regulations impossible then no state could regulate anything.

- Appeal to Emotion: Red Herring — Using parent's suicide tragedy statement to manipulate emotions rather than present logical arguments.

- Genetic Fallacy: Relevance Fallacy — Dismissing criticism based on perceived origin from competitors rather than addressing merit of criticism.

- Tu Quoque: Red Herring — Potential implicit argument that legislators also benefit from tech money so cannot regulate.
