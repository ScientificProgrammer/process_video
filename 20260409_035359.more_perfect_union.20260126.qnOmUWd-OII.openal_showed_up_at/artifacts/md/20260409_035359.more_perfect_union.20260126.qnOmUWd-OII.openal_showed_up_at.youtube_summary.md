# The AI Industry's Political War: How Tech Giants Are Fighting Regulation

## Overview

This investigative report exposes the aggressive lobbying tactics and political influence operations employed by major AI companies—including OpenAI, Meta, and Google—to silence critics, block safety legislation, and prevent government regulation. The video reveals how the industry is using legal intimidation, shadow lobbying groups, and massive super PAC spending to reshape policy in their favor, despite growing public concerns about AI safety.

## The Opening Salvo: Subpoenas and Intimidation

**Tyler Johnston's Story**

Tyler Johnston, who runs a small AI watchdog organization, received subpoenas from OpenAI demanding extensive documentation about his advocacy work, including communications with former employees, congressional offices, and potential investors. The subpoenas revealed OpenAI's true concern: preventing transparency around their controversial restructuring from nonprofit to for-profit status, which involved **$20 billion in investor commitments**.

**OpenAI's Restructuring Battle**

- Founded as a nonprofit by Elon Musk and Sam Altman
- Board attempted to fire Sam Altman, causing investor panic
- Advocates released an open letter asking seven simple questions about restructuring transparency
- OpenAI responded with legal intimidation rather than transparency

## The Influence Machine: Chris Lehane and OpenAI's Political Strategy

**Building the Team**

OpenAI hired **Chris Lehane**, nicknamed the "Master of Disaster" for his crisis management work during the Clinton-Lewinsky scandal. Lehane assembled a powerful team:

- Former staffers of Gavin Newsom, Bill de Blasio, and Kamala Harris
- Republican operative Jeff Miller's lobbying firm (with deep Trump ties)
- Multi-pronged approach: philanthropy, economic threats, and competitor accusations

**The Tactics**

- Threatened to leave California if restructuring was blocked
- Accused critics of being Elon Musk's puppets
- Expanded subpoenas to target documents related to **any AI legislation advocacy**

## California's Legislative Battles

### Rebecca Bauer-Kahan's Three Bills

**California Assembly Member Rebecca Bauer-Kahan introduced multiple AI safety bills:**

1. **Energy Cost Protection**: Preventing data centers from driving up electricity bills for ratepayers
   - Industry response: "You're going to kill the entire AI industry"

2. **Copyright Tracking**: Helping creators identify AI infringement
   - Industry response: "Copyright is not the right lever to pull"

3. **LEAD for Kids Act**: Banning unsafe AI products for children
   - Prompted by teenagers dying by suicide after chatbot interactions
   - OpenAI even subpoenaed parents whose teen died after using ChatGPT

### Shadow Lobbying Groups

**The tech industry operates through multiple intermediary organizations:**

- **TechNet**: Members include Meta, OpenAI, Google, Anthropic
- **Chamber of Progress**: Funded by Andreessen Horowitz, Google, OpenAI
- **American Innovators Network**: Backed by Andreessen Horowitz and Y Combinator

These groups refuse to fully disclose their funding sources while lobbying against safety legislation.

### The Outcome

Despite the **LEAD for Kids Act passing both state chambers with supermajorities**, Governor Gavin Newsom vetoed it. He signed a weaker alternative that only required protocols, not guarantees, for preventing harmful content—giving him political cover while maintaining relationships with the tech industry.

## New York's RAISE Act: A Similar Story

### Alex Bores' Battle

**New York Assembly Member Alex Bores introduced the RAISE Act** to establish legally enforceable safety standards for advanced AI research, addressing extreme harms like AI-enabled bioweapons and applying only to the largest companies.

**Industry Pushback Tactics:**

- Texting constituents directly
- Facebook and Twitter ad campaigns
- Astroturfing and planted op-eds
- Andreessen Horowitz sent alternative bill language that would exempt virtually every company

### The Super PAC Strategy

The AI industry launched **super PACs with hundreds of millions of dollars** to spend on political races:

- Funders: Meta, Andreessen Horowitz, OpenAI co-founder Greg Brockman
- Modeled after **Fairshake**, the crypto super PAC that spent $200 million in 2024
- Same leadership team: Chris Lehane (strategist), Josh Vlasto (spokesman)
- **First target: Alex Bores**, to intimidate governors and scare other candidates

The governor signed Bores' bill but watered down key provisions in line with tech lobby demands.

## The Federal Strategy: Preempting State Regulation

### Trump Administration Alignment

**December Executive Order**: President Trump issued an order threatening to sue states and cut federal funding if they regulate AI—essentially attempting to preempt state-level safety legislation.

**Financial Connections:**

- Greg Brockman and his wife Anna were **top donors to Trump's super PAC** in late 2024
- Industry seeks "light touch" federal regulation to block stronger state laws
- Goal: Achieve liability protection similar to Section 230 for social media

### The Industry's Endgame

**Zero regulation, zero accountability**—making the public bear the costs of AI harms while companies pursue unconstrained development and maximum profits.

## The Growing Backlash and Path Forward

### Public Opinion

**80% of Americans want AI regulation**, driven by diverse concerns:

- Data centers increasing utility bills
- Teenagers harmed by chatbots
- Job displacement
- Misinformation and deepfakes

### Signs of Hope

Despite industry spending, **public pressure is having an effect**:

- OpenAI agreed to support a California ballot initiative for AI child protections
- The measure is stronger than existing legislation (though weaker than the vetoed LEAD for Kids Act)
- Constituent pressure on legislators is proving effective

### The Call to Action

**Key message from advocates:**

"If your vote didn't matter, if your say didn't matter, they wouldn't be spending $100 million to drown it out. This is not hopeless. Demand that the people who represent you are representing you and not representing Big Tech."

## Conclusion

This investigation reveals a comprehensive industry playbook designed to prevent meaningful AI regulation through legal intimidation, shadow lobbying, massive political spending, and federal preemption of state laws. However, the video ends on a note of cautious optimism: **the industry's defensive posture and enormous spending suggest they're actually vulnerable to public pressure**. With 80% of Americans supporting regulation and growing awareness of AI harms, advocates believe grassroots constituent engagement can overcome corporate money and force legislators to prioritize public safety over industry interests. The battle for AI regulation is far from over, and public participation will determine the outcome.
