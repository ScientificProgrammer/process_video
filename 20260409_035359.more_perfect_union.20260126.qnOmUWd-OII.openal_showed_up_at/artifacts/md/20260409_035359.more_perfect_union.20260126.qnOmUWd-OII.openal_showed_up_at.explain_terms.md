# GLOSSARY OF TERMS

- SUBPOENA: A legal document that orders a person or organization to provide testimony or produce documents for a legal proceeding. It carries the force of law and failure to comply can result in penalties including contempt of court.
-- Analogy: A mandatory invitation from a court that you cannot decline, like a summons that requires you to show up with specific items.
-- Why It Matters: OpenAI used subpoenas as an intimidation tactic against small advocacy groups to discover their communications and activities.

- RESTRUCTURING: The process of reorganizing a company's legal, operational, or financial structure, often to change from one type of entity to another. In OpenAI's case, this meant shifting control from its nonprofit board to its for-profit arm.
-- Analogy: Like renovating a house's foundation to change it from a community center into a private residence with different ownership rules.
-- Why It Matters: OpenAI's restructuring was central to protecting 20 billion dollars in investor commitments and sparked the legal battles discussed.

- INFLUENCE OPERATIONS: Coordinated strategic activities designed to affect public opinion, policy decisions, or political outcomes through lobbying, public relations, and political pressure. These operations often involve multiple tactics deployed simultaneously across different channels.
-- Analogy: Like a military campaign, but fought with money, messaging, and political connections instead of weapons.
-- Why It Matters: OpenAI and other AI companies deployed sophisticated influence operations to block regulation and silence critics.

- SHADOW GROUPS: Advocacy or lobbying organizations that appear independent but are actually funded and directed by industry interests to advance corporate agendas. These groups often obscure their true funding sources and corporate connections.
-- Analogy: Puppet theater where the puppets appear to be independent characters, but strings connect them all to the same puppeteer backstage.
-- Why It Matters: Tech companies use shadow groups like TechNet and Chamber of Progress to lobby against regulation while appearing grassroots.

- SUPER PAC: A Political Action Committee that can raise and spend unlimited amounts of money to support or oppose political candidates, but cannot coordinate directly with the candidates' campaigns. Created after the 2010 Citizens United Supreme Court decision.
-- Analogy: A wealthy megaphone that can shout as loudly as it wants about candidates, as long as it doesn't whisper directly in their ear.
-- Why It Matters: AI companies launched super PACs with hundreds of millions to target legislators who support regulation, modeling them after successful crypto industry PACs.

- ASTROTURFING: The practice of creating fake grassroots support for a political position or product by using paid operatives, bots, or coordinated campaigns that appear to be organic citizen movements. Named as the opposite of genuine grassroots organizing.
-- Analogy: Plastic grass that looks real from a distance but is actually manufactured and installed by a company.
-- Why It Matters: AI companies used astroturfing tactics including planted op-eds and coordinated constituent outreach to create false appearance of public opposition to regulation.

- SUPERMAJORITY: A legislative voting threshold that requires more than a simple majority, often two-thirds or three-fifths of votes, to pass legislation or override vetoes. Supermajorities indicate exceptionally strong bipartisan support.
-- Analogy: Not just winning a game, but winning so decisively that nearly everyone on both teams agrees on the outcome.
-- Why It Matters: California's LEAD for Kids Act passed with supermajorities, showing overwhelming support, yet was still vetoed after industry lobbying.

- VETO: The constitutional power of an executive (like a governor or president) to reject legislation passed by a legislature, preventing it from becoming law. Vetoes can sometimes be overridden by legislative supermajorities.
-- Analogy: A referee's power to reverse a decision made by the team captains, sending everyone back to reconsider.
-- Why It Matters: Governor Newsom vetoed strong AI child safety legislation despite bipartisan support, allegedly due to tech industry pressure.

- EXECUTIVE ORDER: A directive issued by the President or governor that has the force of law within the executive branch, though it cannot override existing legislation. Executive orders are often used to direct how laws are enforced or how government operates.
-- Analogy: A manager's memo that tells all employees how to do their jobs, which is binding on the staff but cannot change the company's charter.
-- Why It Matters: Trump issued an executive order threatening states with lawsuits and funding cuts if they regulate AI, attempting to preempt state legislation.

- PREEMPTION: A legal doctrine where higher levels of government (federal) can override or prevent lower levels (state/local) from regulating in certain areas. AI companies seek federal preemption to block state-level AI regulations.
-- Analogy: A parent telling children they cannot make their own bedtime rules because the parent has already set one universal bedtime.
-- Why It Matters: AI companies want federal preemption to establish weak national standards that prevent stricter state laws from taking effect.

- SECTION 230: A provision of the Communications Decency Act that provides immunity to online platforms from liability for content posted by users. Social media companies are not held responsible for what users post on their platforms.
-- Analogy: A bulletin board owner cannot be sued for offensive flyers that other people pin up on the board.
-- Why It Matters: AI companies want similar liability protection for AI-generated content, avoiding responsibility for harms caused by their systems.

- LOBBYING BLITZ: An intensive, coordinated lobbying campaign involving multiple tactics deployed simultaneously including direct legislator contact, advertising, constituent pressure, and media campaigns. Typically involves significant financial resources deployed over a short timeframe.
-- Analogy: A full-court press in basketball where every player aggressively pursues the opponent from all angles simultaneously.
-- Why It Matters: Tech companies launched lobbying blitzes against child safety and AI regulation bills, overwhelming legislators with pressure from multiple directions.

- STARTUP ACCELERATOR: An organization that provides early-stage companies with mentorship, resources, and often funding in exchange for equity, helping them grow rapidly. Y Combinator is one of the most famous accelerators, having launched companies like Airbnb and Dropbox.
-- Analogy: A business bootcamp that takes fledgling companies and puts them through intensive training while investing in their success.
-- Why It Matters: Y Combinator, formerly run by Sam Altman, is part of the network funding anti-regulation lobbying efforts through shadow groups.

- BALLOT INITIATIVE: A process allowing citizens to propose legislation or constitutional amendments directly to voters, bypassing the legislature. If enough signatures are gathered, the measure appears on the ballot for direct democratic vote.
-- Analogy: A petition that, if signed by enough neighbors, forces the entire neighborhood to vote on a new community rule.
-- Why It Matters: After legislative failures, AI child safety advocates turned to a California ballot initiative, forcing OpenAI to publicly support protections.

- ZERO-SUM GAME: A competitive situation where one party's gain is exactly balanced by another party's loss, meaning the total benefit remains constant. The phrase implies that cooperation is impossible because helping others necessarily hurts yourself.
-- Analogy: A pie that cannot grow larger, so if someone takes a bigger slice, everyone else must have smaller slices.
-- Why It Matters: OpenAI's leadership views AI regulation as zero-sum, believing any rules that slow them down give competitors an insurmountable advantage.
