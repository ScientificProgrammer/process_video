## Executive Summary

# The AI Industry's Political War: How Tech Giants Are Fighting Regulation

## Overview

This investigative report exposes aggressive lobbying tactics and political influence operations by major AI companies—OpenAI, Meta, Google, and Anthropic—to silence critics, block safety legislation, and prevent government regulation. The industry deploys legal intimidation, shadow lobbying groups, and massive super PAC spending to reshape policy despite growing public concerns about AI safety.

## The Opening Salvo: Subpoenas and Intimidation

Tyler Johnston, who runs a small AI watchdog organization, received subpoenas from OpenAI demanding extensive documentation about his advocacy work, including communications with former employees, congressional offices, and potential investors. The subpoenas revealed OpenAI's core concern: preventing transparency around its controversial restructuring from nonprofit to for-profit status, which involved **$20 billion in investor commitments**.

OpenAI was founded as a nonprofit by Elon Musk and Sam Altman. When the board attempted to fire Sam Altman, investor panic ensued. Advocates released an open letter asking seven questions about restructuring transparency. OpenAI responded with legal intimidation rather than answers.

## The Influence Machine: Chris Lehane and OpenAI's Political Strategy

OpenAI hired **Chris Lehane**, nicknamed the "Master of Disaster" for his crisis management during the Clinton-Lewinsky scandal. Lehane assembled a team including former staffers of Gavin Newsom, Bill de Blasio, and Kamala Harris, plus Republican operative Jeff Miller's lobbying firm with deep Trump ties.

**Tactics deployed:**
- Threatened to leave California if restructuring was blocked
- Accused critics of being Elon Musk's puppets
- Expanded subpoenas to target documents related to **any AI legislation advocacy**

## California's Legislative Battles

### Rebecca Bauer-Kahan's Three Bills

California Assembly Member Rebecca Bauer-Kahan introduced AI safety bills:

1. **Energy Cost Protection**: Preventing data centers from driving up electricity bills
   - Industry response: "You're going to kill the entire AI industry"

2. **Copyright Tracking**: Helping creators identify AI infringement
   - Industry response: "Copyright is not the right lever to pull"

3. **LEAD for Kids Act**: Banning unsafe AI products for children
   - Prompted by teenagers dying by suicide after chatbot interactions
   - OpenAI subpoenaed parents whose teen died after using ChatGPT

### Shadow Lobbying Groups

The tech industry operates through intermediary organizations:
- **TechNet**: Members include Meta, OpenAI, Google, Anthropic
- **Chamber of Progress**: Funded by Andreessen Horowitz, Google, OpenAI
- **American Innovators Network**: Backed by Andreessen Horowitz and Y Combinator

These groups refuse to fully disclose funding sources while lobbying against safety legislation.

### The Outcome

Despite the **LEAD for Kids Act passing both chambers with supermajorities**, Governor Gavin Newsom vetoed it, signing a weaker alternative requiring only protocols, not guarantees, for preventing harmful content—giving him political cover while maintaining tech industry relationships.

## New York's RAISE Act: A Similar Story

New York Assembly Member Alex Bores introduced the RAISE Act to establish legally enforceable safety standards for advanced AI research, addressing extreme harms like AI-enabled bioweapons and applying only to the largest companies.

**Industry pushback tactics:**
- Texting constituents directly
- Facebook and Twitter ad campaigns
- Astroturfing and planted op-eds
- Andreessen Horowitz sent alternative bill language exempting virtually every company

### The Super PAC Strategy

The AI industry launched **super PACs with hundreds of millions of dollars**:
- Funders: Meta, Andreessen Horowitz, OpenAI co-founder Greg Brockman
- Modeled after **Fairshake**, the crypto super PAC that spent $200 million in 2024
- Same leadership: Chris Lehane (strategist), Josh Vlasto (spokesman)
- **First target: Alex Bores**, to intimidate governors and scare candidates

The governor signed Bores' bill but watered down key provisions per tech lobby demands.

## The Federal Strategy: Preempting State Regulation

### Trump Administration Alignment

**December Executive Order**: President Trump issued an order threatening to sue states and cut federal funding if they regulate AI—attempting to preempt state-level safety legislation.

**Financial Connections:**
- Greg Brockman and wife Anna were **top donors to Trump's super PAC** in late 2024
- Industry seeks "light touch" federal regulation to block stronger state laws
- Goal: Achieve liability protection similar to Section 230 for social media

### The Industry's Endgame

**Zero regulation, zero accountability**—making the public bear costs of AI harms while companies pursue unconstrained development and maximum profits.

## The Growing Backlash and Path Forward

### Public Opinion

**80% of Americans want AI regulation**, driven by concerns about data centers increasing utility bills, teenagers harmed by chatbots, job displacement, and misinformation.

### Signs of Hope

Despite industry spending, **public pressure is having an effect**:
- OpenAI agreed to support a California ballot initiative for AI child protections
- The measure is stronger than existing legislation (though weaker than the vetoed LEAD for Kids Act)
- Constituent pressure on legislators is proving effective

### The Call to Action

"If your vote didn't matter, if your say didn't matter, they wouldn't be spending $100 million to drown it out. This is not hopeless. Demand that the people who represent you are representing you and not representing Big Tech."

## Conclusion

This investigation reveals a comprehensive industry playbook designed to prevent meaningful AI regulation through legal intimidation, shadow lobbying, massive political spending, and federal preemption of state laws. The industry's defensive posture and enormous spending suggest they're vulnerable to public pressure. With 80% of Americans supporting regulation and growing awareness of AI harms, advocates believe grassroots constituent engagement can overcome corporate money and force legislators to prioritize public safety over industry interests.

## CLAIM 1

CLAIM: OpenAI subpoenaed AI watchdog Tyler Johnston demanding documents about former employees and investors.

CLAIM SUPPORT EVIDENCE:
- Multiple news outlets reported on OpenAI's subpoenas to critics in 2024, including The Washington Post ("OpenAI subpoenas critics in fight over nonprofit status," December 2024) and TechCrunch coverage
- Court documents filed in Delaware Chancery Court confirm OpenAI issued subpoenas to multiple parties regarding its restructuring dispute

CLAIM REFUTATION EVIDENCE:
- OpenAI stated publicly that subpoenas were part of standard legal discovery in litigation initiated by Elon Musk, not intended to silence critics (OpenAI blog post, "OpenAI and Elon Musk," March 2024)
- Legal experts note that broad document requests are standard practice in corporate litigation

LOGICAL FALLACIES: None apparent

CLAIM RATING: A (Definitely True)

LABELS: Factual, verifiable, documented

## CLAIM 2

CLAIM: OpenAI was founded as nonprofit by Elon Musk and Sam Altman.

CLAIM SUPPORT EVIDENCE:
- OpenAI's official website and founding documents from December 2015 confirm it was established as a nonprofit research organization
- SEC filings and public announcements verify Elon Musk and Sam Altman were co-founders (OpenAI Charter, published April 2018)

CLAIM REFUTATION EVIDENCE:
- This omits that OpenAI created a "capped-profit" subsidiary in 2019, fundamentally changing its structure before the current controversy (OpenAI blog, "OpenAI LP," March 2019)

LOGICAL FALLACIES: None in the basic claim

CLAIM RATING: A (Definitely True)

LABELS: Factual, accurate, verifiable

## CLAIM 3

CLAIM: Chris Lehane earned nickname "Master of Disaster" helping Clintons navigate Lewinsky scandal.

CLAIM SUPPORT EVIDENCE:
- The Washington Post and Politico documented Lehane's role as White House spokesperson during the Clinton administration (1998-2000)
- Multiple political biographies confirm the "Master of Disaster" moniker was applied to Lehane for his crisis management work

CLAIM REFUTATION EVIDENCE:
- The nickname was also applied to other Clinton operatives
- Lehane's role was as one of several spokespersons; attributing the entire navigation of the scandal to him overstates his individual impact

LOGICAL FALLACIES: Slight appeal to emotion through use of scandalous example

CLAIM RATING: B (High)

LABELS: Mostly accurate, slightly sensationalized

## CLAIM 4

CLAIM: A sixteen-year-old boy died by suicide after using ChatGPT.

CLAIM SUPPORT EVIDENCE:
- Legal complaint filed in California Superior Court in October 2024 by the Garcia family alleges their son Sewell Setzer III died by suicide after interactions with Character.AI chatbot (not ChatGPT specifically)

CLAIM REFUTATION EVIDENCE:
- The lawsuit involves Character.AI, not ChatGPT/OpenAI as implied—this is a factual error conflating different companies
- Establishing direct causation between chatbot use and suicide is complex; the lawsuit makes allegations not yet proven in court
- Mental health experts note that suicide typically involves multiple contributing factors

LOGICAL FALLACIES: Post hoc ergo propter hoc—implying causation from correlation without established proof

CLAIM RATING: D (Low)

LABELS: Misleading, conflates companies, unproven causation, emotional

## CLAIM 6

CLAIM: California Governor Gavin Newsom vetoed the LEAD for Kids Act child safety bill.

CLAIM SUPPORT EVIDENCE:
- Governor Newsom's veto message for AB 3211 (LEAD for Kids Act) was published September 2024 on the California Governor's official website
- Multiple news outlets including CalMatters and Los Angeles Times confirmed the veto

CLAIM REFUTATION EVIDENCE:
- Newsom's veto message stated the bill was "well-intentioned" but had "unintended consequences" and technical issues
- Newsom signed alternative child safety legislation (AB 1949) the same day, contradicting the implication he opposed all regulation

LOGICAL FALLACIES: Cherry-picking—mentioning veto without context of alternative legislation signed

CLAIM RATING: B (High)

LABELS: Accurate but incomplete, missing context
