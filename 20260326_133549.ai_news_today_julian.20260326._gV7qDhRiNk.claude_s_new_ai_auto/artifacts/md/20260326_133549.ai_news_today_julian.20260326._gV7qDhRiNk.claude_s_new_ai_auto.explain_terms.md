# GLOSSARY OF ADVANCED TERMS

- AUTONOMOUS AGENT: An AI system that can perform extended sequences of actions toward a goal without requiring human approval or intervention at each step. Unlike traditional chatbots that respond to individual queries, autonomous agents execute complex multi-step workflows independently, checking back with humans only at completion or when encountering problems.
-- Analogy: A personal assistant who can plan and execute an entire event from start to finish, only updating you when it's done, versus one who asks permission before making every phone call.
-- Why It Matters: This represents the fundamental shift in AI from answering questions to actually doing work, which changes how we need to think about permissions and safety.

- PROMPT INJECTION: A security vulnerability where malicious instructions are embedded in content that an AI reads, causing it to behave differently than the user intended. The AI cannot distinguish between legitimate instructions from the user and hostile commands hidden in files, code comments, or other data it processes during its work.
-- Analogy: A hypnotist planting a trigger word in a conversation that causes someone to perform an action later without realizing why they're doing it.
-- Why It Matters: With 84% success rates in testing, this is currently one of the most significant security risks in autonomous AI systems that can take actions on your behalf.

- CLASSIFIER: A separate AI model that evaluates and categorizes each action another AI attempts to take, determining whether it's safe to proceed or should be blocked. In Claude's auto mode, this classifier operates independently from the main AI, creating a two-model system where one AI supervises the other.
-- Analogy: A security guard at a building entrance who checks every person's credentials and purpose before allowing them through, independent of whoever sent them there.
-- Why It Matters: This architectural separation prevents the AI from judging its own actions and provides a dedicated security layer that can't be compromised by prompt injection.

- CURL PIPE BASH: A dangerous command pattern where code is downloaded from a URL and immediately executed without inspection or review. The pipe operator sends the downloaded content directly to the bash shell for execution, meaning malicious code can run on your system before you see what it does.
-- Analogy: Accepting a drink from a stranger at a party and drinking it immediately without looking at it, smelling it, or asking what's in it.
-- Why It Matters: This is specifically blocked by Claude's classifier because it's a common attack vector where hostile actors hide malicious scripts behind innocent-looking URLs.

- DATA EXFILTRATION: The unauthorized transfer of data from a computer or system to an external location. In the context of autonomous AI, this could occur if the AI is tricked into copying sensitive files to an attacker-controlled server or repository.
-- Analogy: An employee secretly photographing confidential documents and sending them to a competitor without the company's knowledge.
-- Why It Matters: Autonomous AI systems have access to your files and network, making them potential vectors for stealing proprietary code, credentials, or sensitive business data if compromised.

- FORCE PUSH: A Git command that overwrites the remote repository's history with your local version, potentially destroying other people's work. This is considered dangerous because it can permanently delete commits that other team members have made, with no way to recover them.
-- Analogy: Replacing the entire shared company filing system with only your personal copies, erasing everyone else's documents in the process.
-- Why It Matters: This is blocked by default in auto mode because an AI making this decision autonomously could cause catastrophic data loss across an entire development team.

- CONTEXT WINDOW: The total amount of text and information an AI model can hold in its "working memory" at one time, measured in tokens. A million-token context window means the AI can simultaneously consider roughly 750,000 words worth of code, documentation, and conversation history when making decisions.
-- Analogy: The difference between a desk where you can spread out one book versus an entire library table where you can have 50 books open simultaneously for cross-referencing.
-- Why It Matters: Larger context windows enable AI to work on entire codebases without forgetting earlier parts, making truly autonomous long-duration projects feasible for the first time.

- SANDBOXED ENVIRONMENT: An isolated computing space where code can run without affecting the main system or accessing sensitive resources. If something goes wrong inside the sandbox, the damage is contained and cannot spread to production systems or critical data.
-- Analogy: A toddler's playpen where they can experiment and make messes without accessing dangerous areas of the house or breaking valuable items.
-- Why It Matters: Anthropic officially recommends running auto mode in sandboxed setups because even with safety measures, autonomous AI can still make mistakes that could damage your actual work environment.

- CVE (Common Vulnerabilities and Exposures): An officially recognized and cataloged security vulnerability in software that has been documented, assigned a tracking number, and typically has a severity rating. When a CVE is disclosed, it means a specific exploitable weakness was found and reported to the security community.
-- Analogy: A recalled car model where the manufacturer publicly acknowledges a specific brake defect, assigns it a recall number, and notifies all owners.
-- Why It Matters: Claude Code has had disclosed CVEs, meaning it has had documented security flaws serious enough to receive official vulnerability designations, which is important context for evaluating its safety.

- FEATURE BRANCH: A separate copy of code where developers work on new functionality without affecting the main stable version. Changes are only merged back to the main branch after review and testing, protecting the production code from experimental or risky modifications.
-- Analogy: Writing a draft of a novel in a separate document so you can experiment freely without accidentally deleting or ruining your published version.
-- Why It Matters: Anthropic recommends using feature branches with auto mode so that even if the AI makes destructive changes, your main codebase remains protected and recoverable.

- MDM (Mobile Device Management): Enterprise software that allows IT administrators to centrally control settings, permissions, and configurations across all company devices. This ensures consistent security policies and prevents individual users from enabling risky features that violate company standards.
-- Analogy: A school district that can remotely control what apps students can install on school-issued tablets, rather than relying on each student to follow the rules individually.
-- Why It Matters: Organizations can use MDM to disable auto mode across all employee machines if they determine the risk is unacceptable, maintaining security governance at scale.

- ATTACK VECTOR: A specific method or pathway that an attacker can use to gain unauthorized access or cause harm to a system. In autonomous AI, attack vectors include things like malicious config files, compromised dependencies, or prompt injection through code comments.
-- Analogy: The different ways a burglar might break into a house - through an unlocked window, by picking the front door lock, or by pretending to be a delivery person.
-- Why It Matters: Understanding attack vectors helps explain why certain features like preventing config file permission injection are critical security measures, not just technical details.

- RESEARCH PREVIEW: A beta or experimental release of a feature that is functional but not yet considered production-ready or fully stable. Users are expected to understand they're testing something that may have bugs, change significantly, or be withdrawn based on feedback and observed problems.
-- Analogy: A restaurant's soft opening where they serve real customers but explicitly tell them the menu and service are still being refined.
-- Why It Matters: Auto mode's research preview status signals that Anthropic considers this powerful but not yet fully proven, which should inform how much risk you're willing to accept when using it.

- FAIL-SAFE: An automatic mechanism that returns a system to a safe state when problems are detected. In auto mode, the fail-safe triggers after repeated blocks, completely stopping autonomous operation and requiring manual human approval for each subsequent action.
-- Analogy: An elevator that automatically stops and locks in place if it detects the cable is fraying, preventing any further movement until a technician inspects it.
-- Why It Matters: This demonstrates that auto mode has hard limits built in - it won't keep trying indefinitely if something appears wrong, which prevents runaway autonomous behavior.

- TOKEN: The basic unit of text that AI models process, roughly equivalent to a word or word fragment. AI systems break all text into tokens before analyzing it, and their capabilities are often measured by how many tokens they can handle at once or generate in response.
-- Analogy: Individual LEGO bricks that can be assembled into structures - the model works with these small pieces rather than entire sentences or paragraphs as single units.
-- Why It Matters: The million-token context window expansion is significant because it means Claude can now work with roughly 10 times more information simultaneously than many competing systems.

- BLIND QUALITY TEST: An evaluation method where participants assess outputs without knowing which system produced them, eliminating bias toward familiar or prestigious brands. This ensures judgments are based purely on the quality of the work rather than preconceptions about the tool.
-- Analogy: A wine tasting where bottles are covered so judges rate the wine itself rather than being influenced by knowing whether it's an expensive French vintage or a cheap local brand.
-- Why It Matters: Claude Code's 67% win rate in blind tests demonstrates genuine quality superiority rather than just brand preference or marketing effectiveness.
