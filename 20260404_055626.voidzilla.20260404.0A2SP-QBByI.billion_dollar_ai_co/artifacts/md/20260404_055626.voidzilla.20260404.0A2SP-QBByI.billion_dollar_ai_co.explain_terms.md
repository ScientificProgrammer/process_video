# GLOSSARY OF TERMS

- TELEHEALTH: A method of delivering healthcare services remotely using telecommunications technology, allowing patients to consult with medical providers through digital platforms rather than in-person visits. This model became particularly popular during the COVID-19 pandemic and has enabled companies to scale medical services rapidly without physical infrastructure.
-- Analogy: Like having a doctor's appointment through a video call instead of driving to the clinic.
-- Why It Matters: Telehealth enables rapid business scaling but also creates opportunities for less regulated medical practices and oversight gaps.

- GLP-1 DRUGS: Glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor agonists are a class of medications originally developed for type 2 diabetes that have become popular for weight loss by mimicking a hormone that regulates appetite and blood sugar. These drugs include brand names like Ozempic, Wegovy, and Zepbound and have created a massive market due to their effectiveness.
-- Analogy: Like a chemical messenger that tells your brain you're full after eating less food.
-- Why It Matters: The massive demand for these weight loss drugs created a lucrative market that attracted both legitimate providers and fraudulent operators.

- ONE-PERSON BILLION-DOLLAR FIRM: A theoretical business concept popularized by AI advocates suggesting that artificial intelligence tools could enable a single individual or tiny team to build and operate a company worth over a billion dollars. This idea represents the extreme end of AI-enabled business automation and productivity gains.
-- Analogy: Like one person with power tools doing the work that previously required an entire construction crew.
-- Why It Matters: This concept drives venture capital interest and media narratives around AI but may overlook ethical shortcuts taken to achieve such scale.

- CLASS ACTION LAWSUIT: A legal proceeding where one or several plaintiffs file a lawsuit on behalf of a larger group of people who have suffered similar harm from the same defendant. This legal mechanism allows individuals with relatively small claims to collectively seek justice against large corporations.
-- Analogy: Like neighbors joining together to sue a company that damaged all their properties rather than each filing separately.
-- Why It Matters: Class action lawsuits serve as a major accountability mechanism for companies engaging in widespread deceptive practices.

- FDA WARNING LETTER: An official communication from the U.S. Food and Drug Administration notifying a company that it has violated federal regulations, typically requiring corrective action to avoid further enforcement like product seizures or injunctions. These letters are public documents that signal serious regulatory concerns about a company's practices.
-- Analogy: Like receiving a formal written warning from a building inspector before they shut down your restaurant.
-- Why It Matters: FDA warning letters indicate serious regulatory violations and often precede more severe enforcement actions if not addressed.

- SNAKE OIL: A term originating from fraudulent 19th-century medicine shows, now referring to any product marketed with exaggerated or false claims about its effectiveness, particularly in medical or health contexts. The term implies deliberate deception for profit rather than honest mistakes.
-- Analogy: Like selling colored water as a miracle cure while knowing it has no medical properties.
-- Why It Matters: Snake oil products exploit vulnerable consumers seeking health solutions and represent a form of fraud with potential health consequences.

- COMPOUNDED DRUGS: Medications that are custom-mixed by pharmacists to create formulations not commercially available, often to meet specific patient needs like different dosages or delivery methods. While legitimate compounding serves important medical purposes, the practice can also be exploited to create unapproved versions of popular medications.
-- Analogy: Like a chef creating a custom recipe instead of serving a pre-packaged meal from a manufacturer.
-- Why It Matters: Compounding allows companies to sell versions of popular drugs that bypass normal FDA approval processes for commercial medications.

- TIRZEPATIDE: A dual GIP/GLP-1 receptor agonist medication approved by the FDA as an injectable treatment for type 2 diabetes and obesity, sold under brand names like Mounjaro and Zepbound. The compound works through two hormone pathways to regulate blood sugar and appetite, making it highly effective for weight loss.
-- Analogy: Like a key that fits two different locks simultaneously to activate complementary weight loss mechanisms.
-- Why It Matters: While effective as an injection, the oral formulation mentioned in the content allegedly has no proven efficacy or absorption mechanism.

- PHARMACOLOGICALLY INERT: A substance that produces no pharmacological effect in the body, meaning it has no active biological or therapeutic impact regardless of dosage. When a compound is inert in a particular delivery method, it passes through the body without being absorbed or producing the intended medical effect.
-- Analogy: Like trying to charge your phone by placing the charger near it without plugging it in.
-- Why It Matters: Selling pharmacologically inert products as effective medications constitutes fraud and can harm patients who forego legitimate treatments.

- AI SLOP: A derogatory term for low-quality, mass-produced content generated by artificial intelligence, typically characterized by obvious fakeness, lack of authenticity, and creation at scale for deceptive purposes. This includes fake images, videos, testimonials, and other content designed to mislead consumers.
-- Analogy: Like spam emails but extended to images, videos, and entire fake personas flooding the internet.
-- Why It Matters: AI slop represents the misuse of generative AI technology to create deceptive marketing at unprecedented scale and low cost.

- META ADS LIBRARY: A publicly searchable database maintained by Meta (Facebook's parent company) that displays all active ads running across Facebook and Instagram, including advertiser information and ad content. This transparency tool was created to allow researchers and the public to examine advertising practices and identify problematic campaigns.
-- Analogy: Like a public bulletin board where you can see every advertisement a company is currently running in the city.
-- Why It Matters: The ads library enables journalists and researchers to document deceptive advertising practices that companies might otherwise deny.

- AFFILIATES: Third-party marketers who promote a company's products in exchange for commission on sales they generate, often using their own marketing channels and methods. While legitimate affiliate marketing is common, companies sometimes use affiliates to create plausible deniability for deceptive marketing practices.
-- Analogy: Like independent salespeople who sell products door-to-door and keep a percentage of each sale.
-- Why It Matters: Companies may blame affiliates for deceptive practices while benefiting from the sales those practices generate, creating accountability gaps.

- SEMAGLUTIDE: A GLP-1 receptor agonist medication approved for treating type 2 diabetes and obesity, marketed under brand names like Ozempic and Wegovy. Similar to tirzepatide, semaglutide works by mimicking hormones that regulate appetite and has become extremely popular for weight loss.
-- Analogy: Like an earlier version of the same technology that unlocks appetite control in the brain.
-- Why It Matters: Semaglutide's popularity created market demand that led to both legitimate telehealth providers and fraudulent operations selling questionable formulations.
