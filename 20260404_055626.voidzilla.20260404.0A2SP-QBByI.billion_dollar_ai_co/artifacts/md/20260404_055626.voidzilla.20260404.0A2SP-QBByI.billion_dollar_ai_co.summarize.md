# ONE SENTENCE SUMMARY:

A viral New York Times article praised Medvy's AI-powered success while ignoring fraud, fake doctors, and ineffective products.

# MAIN POINTS:

1. Medvy reached $1.8 billion valuation using AI, celebrated by New York Times and Sam Altman.
2. The company used fake before-and-after photos, modified with AI or stolen from Reddit.
3. Fake AI-generated doctors promoted Medvy products in Facebook ads still running today.
4. Real doctors denied involvement after their names appeared on Medvy's website with different photos.
5. Oral tirzepatide tablets allegedly function as snake oil with no proven efficacy or approval.
6. Eli Lilly states no human studies exist for oral tirzepatide, making claims experimental and unproven.
7. FDA issued warning letter for false and misleading claims about compounded semaglutide and tirzepatide.
8. Customer reviews report no weight loss, defective products, and shady billing practices without refunds.
9. Fake testimonials were hosted directly on Medvy's website as recently as March 2025.
10. Futurism's investigative journalism exposing the fraud received far less attention than Times' puff piece.

# TAKEAWAYS:

1. AI enables fraud to scale faster than regulation can respond, creating billion-dollar scams.
2. Mainstream media may prioritize compelling narratives over investigating fraudulent business practices behind revenue numbers.
3. Consumers cannot trust online medical testimonials, doctor endorsements, or before-and-after photos without verification.
4. Oral formulations of injectable drugs may be pharmacologically inert despite aggressive marketing claims.
5. Investigative journalism exposing corporate fraud deserves more attention than promotional success stories celebrating questionable companies.
