# FALLACIES

- Slippery Slope: Informal Fallacy — Claiming AI will inevitably lead to all doctors and lawyers being fake online.

- Hasty Generalization: Faulty Generalization — Concluding all AI business success stories are fraudulent based on one example.

- Appeal to Emotion: Red Herring Fallacy — Using phrases like "experimenting on unsuspecting Americans" to generate fear and outrage.

- Straw Man: Relevance Fallacy — Misrepresenting the New York Times article as purely celebratory without critical examination.

- False Dilemma: False Attribution — Presenting only two options: legitimate company or complete scam, ignoring nuanced possibilities.

- Guilt by Association: Association Fallacy — Linking all AI-enabled businesses to fraud because of Medvy's alleged practices.

- Cherry Picking: Faulty Generalization — Selecting only negative customer reviews while potentially ignoring any positive customer experiences.

- Appeal to Authority: Red Herring Fallacy — Citing Eli Lilly's statement as definitive without acknowledging speaker's admitted non-expertise.

- Proof by Assertion: Improper Premise — Repeatedly stating "this whole thing is built on lies" without comprehensive evidence.

- Argument from Incredulity: Relevance Fallacy — Suggesting AI success must involve deception because legitimate billion-dollar growth seems implausible.

- Poisoning the Well: Ad Hominem — Framing the New York Times as uncritical before presenting counter-evidence.

- Texas Sharpshooter: Relevance Fallacy — Selecting specific Medvy practices to represent all AI-enabled business models universally.
